| 2026-08-02 | P1.2 | **PRE-COMMITTED reading of the 600-iter probe's validation trajectory, recorded before the run was started.** Baseline is the untrained val loss measured at iter 1. Evals land at iters 200, 400, 600. **5e-5 is RIGHT** if val falls monotonically across all three and the 400→600 drop is smaller than the 200→400 drop — decelerating but still descending, with no eval above its predecessor. Proceed to 2,956 iters unchanged. **5e-5 is TOO HIGH** if any eval rises above the one before it, or if a train-loss report returns `nan` or `inf`, or if val at 600 is not below val at 200. Retry the probe at 2e-5 before any long run. **5e-5 is TOO LOW** if val falls monotonically but the total drop from baseline to iter 600 is under 15%, or if the 400→600 drop is within noise of the 200→400 drop (still linear, not yet bending). Retry the probe at 1e-4. **Ambiguous** — non-monotonic but never rising above baseline, or a drop between 15% and 25% — is treated as TOO LOW, because 2,956 iters is 5x the probe and an LR that has barely moved in 600 iters will not be rescued by length | Read the three numbers in the morning and judge; or skip the probe and run 2,956 iters at 5e-5 | Same reasoning as the hand-review acceptance rule and `config.decide_k()`. The pressure here is one-directional and large: the alternative to "the LR looks fine" is redoing an 18-hour matched pair, so *any* descending curve will look acceptable at 8am. Writing the bands first is what stops "it went down" from becoming the whole analysis. The 15% and 25% thresholds are deliberately crude — with three eval points there is no basis for anything finer, and a precise-looking threshold would imply precision the measurement does not have |
| 2026-08-02 | P1.2 | The probe changes **only `iters`** (600 against 2,956). Every other knob is the value the full run will use | Probe cheaply at a shorter sequence length or a smaller batch to save time | A probe that differs in seq length, precision, layer count or accumulation measures a different optimisation problem and tells you nothing about the LR you will actually use. The only reason 600 is safe to vary is that it is a prefix of the same schedule — constant LR means iters 1-600 of the probe are exactly iters 1-600 of the real run, so the probe is literally the first 1.7 hours of v1 rather than a simulation of it |

| 2026-08-02 | P1.2 | **v1 and v2 hold optimizer steps identical and let the mix change what those steps see** — same `iters`, LR, rank, layers, seq length; only the data differs | Hold *epochs* equal, so v2's smaller set gets proportionally fewer steps | BUILD_PLAN requires the v1→v2 regression to be attributable to the data mix and documented as such. | 2026-08-02 | P1.2 | **Batch size stays 1 with gradient accumulation, not 2.** This closes the throughput question left open last session | `--batch-size 2` at a shorter sequence length | Measured: batch 2 fits comfortably on a *typical* batch (9.73 GB at 3,072 tokens) and blows the 12.71 GB ceiling on a long one (**18.69 GB at 6,528**). `iterate_batches` sorts by length, so the long batch is not a tail risk, it is a certainty once per epoch. | 2026-08-02 | P1.2 | LoRA applies to **16 layers at rank 8** — mlx-lm's defaults for both, but chosen here on measurement, not inheritance | 8 layers (the smoke value), or all 28 at rank 16 | Going 8→16 layers costs 0.38 GB (10.60 → 10.98) and doubles trainable params to 4.98M. Rank 16 at 16 layers is nearly free on memory (+0.02 GB) and is the first knob to raise if v1 underfits |
| 2026-08-02 | P1.2 | **Constant learning rate, no decay schedule**, at 5e-5 | mlx-lm's 1e-5 default; or cosine decay, which usually converges better | Resume drives this. `--resume-adapter-file` restores adapter weights and nothing else — not optimizer moments, not the data iterator, not schedule position — so a cosine schedule silently restarts from its peak if the run dies at hour 3 of nine.
